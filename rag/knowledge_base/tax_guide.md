# Income Tax Guide (India, FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27)

## New tax regime (default)
Slabs on taxable income: 0-4L nil; 4-8L 5%; 8-12L 10%; 12-16L 15%; 16-20L 20%; 20-24L 25%; above 24L 30%. A 4% health & education cess applies on the tax.
Standard deduction for salaried is Rs 75,000. Section 87A gives a rebate up to Rs 60,000, so taxable income up to Rs 12 lakh means zero tax. For salaried people that means salary up to about Rs 12.75 lakh is effectively tax-free. Marginal relief softens the jump just above Rs 12 lakh. The new regime does NOT allow 80C, 80D, or HRA deductions.

## Old tax regime (optional)
Slabs: 0-2.5L nil; 2.5-5L 5%; 5-10L 20%; above 10L 30%, plus 4% cess. Standard deduction is Rs 50,000. Section 87A rebate is up to Rs 12,500, so taxable income up to Rs 5 lakh means zero tax. The old regime allows deductions like 80C (up to Rs 1.5 lakh), 80D (health insurance premium), HRA, and home loan interest.

## Which regime to choose
The new regime is better for most people who do not claim large deductions. The old regime can win only if your total deductions are large (typically above Rs 3.5-4 lakh) from 80C, 80D, HRA, and home loan interest combined. Always compute both and pick the lower tax. Do not lock money into low-return instruments purely to save tax; the lost returns usually cost more than the tax saved.

## Common deductions (old regime)
80C covers EPF, PPF, ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premium, principal on home loan, and children's tuition, capped at Rs 1.5 lakh. 80D covers health insurance premium. 80CCD(1B) gives an extra Rs 50,000 for NPS. HRA exemption depends on rent paid and city.
