# Retirement & FIRE Guide (India)

## How big should the corpus be
Estimate your annual expenses at retirement by inflating today's expenses (assume about 6% inflation) to your retirement age. Then multiply by 25 to 30. The 25x figure comes from the 4% rule: if you withdraw 4% of the corpus in the first year and adjust for inflation after, a diversified portfolio has historically lasted 30-plus years. Indian planners often prefer 30x for a bigger safety margin, because Indian inflation is higher and life expectancy is rising.

## The SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan)
After retirement, park the corpus in a mix of debt and moderate equity earning about 7-9% and withdraw monthly through an SWP. As long as the withdrawal rate stays near or below the real (after-inflation) return, the corpus can last indefinitely. Withdrawing too aggressively, or a bad sequence of early market losses, can drain it early. A bucket strategy helps: keep 2-3 years of expenses in cash or debt so you never sell equity in a downturn.

## Reaching the target
The required monthly SIP depends on years to retirement and expected return. Starting early matters enormously because of compounding: the same corpus needs a far smaller SIP at 30 than at 45. Increase the SIP every year as income grows (step-up), and route any windfalls (bonus, appraisal) into investments rather than lifestyle inflation.

## The biggest lever
For most people the single biggest lever on retiring early is income growth, not squeezing expenses. A higher income, invested at the same savings rate, compresses the timeline dramatically. Career growth and skills that raise earning power are themselves a retirement strategy.
