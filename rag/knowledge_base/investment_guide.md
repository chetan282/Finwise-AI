# Investment Guide (India)

## Priority order (do these in sequence)
First, build an emergency fund of 6 months of expenses in a savings account or liquid fund. Second, clear high-interest debt such as personal loans and credit cards (14-40% interest) because paying these off is a guaranteed return. Third, buy adequate term life and health insurance. Only then invest for long-term goals.

## Asset allocation by horizon
For goals more than 7 years away (like retirement), lean equity-heavy through index funds and diversified equity mutual funds. For 3-7 year goals, use a mix of equity and debt (hybrid funds). For goals under 3 years, stay in debt: FDs, liquid funds, and short-duration debt funds. A simple age-based rule of thumb: equity percentage around (100 minus your age), adjusted for risk appetite.

## SIP and compounding
A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount monthly. Rupee-cost averaging smooths out market timing. Equity mutual funds have historically returned about 11-13% per year over long periods, though returns are never guaranteed. The earlier you start, the more compounding does the heavy lifting; a rupee invested at 30 is worth far more at 55 than a rupee invested at 40.

## Instruments
Index funds (Nifty 50, Nifty Next 50) offer low-cost broad equity exposure. ELSS funds give equity growth plus 80C tax benefit with a 3-year lock-in. PPF is a safe 15-year debt instrument with tax-free returns. EPF is automatic for salaried employees. NPS is a low-cost retirement product with an extra 80CCD(1B) tax deduction. Direct stocks carry higher risk and need research.

## Behaviour
The biggest destroyer of returns is panic-selling in downturns and stopping SIPs. Stay invested, keep contributions automatic, and increase the SIP amount every time income rises (step-up SIP).
